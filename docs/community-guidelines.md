# United Feminist — Community Guidelines

*Effective date: October 1, 2026*

---

## Who We Are and Why These Guidelines Exist

United Feminist is a social platform built for women and for the people who stand with them — including trans women, who are a full and welcome part of this community. Full stop.

We opened this platform to anyone genuinely committed to its purpose, because we believe the work of building feminist community does not benefit from shutting out allies. A safe space for women is not measured by who it excludes — it is defined by what it demands. Everyone here is expected to show up as an ally. The question is whether you actually do.

Members arrive through a community-grounded admission process: either someone who knows you personally has vouched for you and actively confirmed it, or your application has been reviewed for good-faith signals and admitted that way. Either way, a place here means the community has a reasonable basis for trust in you.

These guidelines exist to protect that space. They are not an exhaustive legal document — that is what our Terms of Service are for. This is the human version: what we expect from you, what you can expect from us, and what happens when things go wrong.

We have written these guidelines with respect for you as an adult. We will not lecture you about things you already know. But we will be direct about where the lines are and what happens when they are crossed.

---

## The Community We Are Building

United Feminist is a place to post, connect, organize, share, and exist online without the constant background noise of harassment that defines so much of the internet. Women built it, women center it, and women's safety is its purpose. It is open to anyone who takes that purpose seriously.

This platform is inclusive of all women, including:
- Trans women — you are not a special case or an asterisk; you belong here as fully as anyone else
- Women of every race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age, disability status, and background
- Women across the spectrum of gender expression — femme, butch, androgynous, nonconforming, and everything in between

Men and people of any gender who are committed to this community's values are welcome here as allies. What being an ally means in practice is described in the section below.

Membership is open to anyone willing to abide by these guidelines. New members either join through the vouching process — where someone who knows them personally actively confirms their membership — or through a review of their application for good-faith signals. No one's appearance is evaluated. There is no gender screening. The only basis for admission is whether the community has a reasonable foundation for trust in you. The only basis for removal is your conduct.

## What It Means to Be an Ally Here

If you are not a woman and you are here, you are here in service of something larger than yourself. That is not a burden — it is the whole point. Here is what it looks like in practice.

**Listen more than you speak.**
This is a space where women's voices lead. You can contribute, add to conversations, and share your perspective. But if you find yourself consistently at the center of threads, redirecting discussions toward your own experience, or requiring the community's attention on your opinions, you are not showing up as an ally.

**Do not centre yourself.**
When a conversation touches on women's experiences, your response should not redirect to your own. You can relate — but relating is different from redirecting. Adding to a conversation and taking it over are not the same thing.

**Amplify, do not override.**
Reshare, support, and lift up the voices you came here to stand behind. If you disagree with someone, engage respectfully and accept that this community's purpose takes precedence over winning the argument.

**Do not treat this space as an audience.**
You are here to participate in a community, not to observe it from a distance or broadcast to it. The women here did not build this platform to be watched.

**These expectations apply to your conduct, not your identity.**
You are not on probation. You are not under special surveillance. If you are here in good faith and you act in good faith, you belong here. The same guidelines apply to everyone. The same enforcement applies to everyone. What matters — the only thing that matters — is what you do.

## What We Expect From You

### Be who you say you are
Your account must reflect your real name. You may post under your chosen @handle, but your profile carries your actual name. Impersonating someone else — a public figure, a private individual, another member, or the platform itself — is not allowed.

### Interact with good faith
Disagreement is fine. Debate is fine. Anger is sometimes appropriate. But there is a difference between disagreeing with someone and targeting them. Know the difference and stay on the right side of it.

### Respect that other members chose to be here
Everyone here arrived through trust. Treat other members accordingly — not with forced positivity, but with basic recognition that they are real people, not content.

## What Is Not Allowed

The following are violations of these guidelines. Each one is prohibited, and each one carries consequences. We have described what each category actually looks like in practice, because "cyberbullying is prohibited" without examples tells you almost nothing.

---

### 1. Cyberbullying

Bullying is the use of the platform to deliberately harm, humiliate, or intimidate another person — not as an isolated comment, but as a pattern or campaign.

**What this looks like:**
- Repeatedly targeting the same person across multiple posts, threads, or DMs to wear her down
- Coordinating with others to flood someone's mentions or overwhelm her notifications
- Mocking someone's appearance, disability, health condition, or personal circumstances in ways clearly designed to cause distress
- Screenshotting someone's posts and sharing them in a mocking context within the platform
- Creating posts specifically designed to humiliate a named individual

A one-time comment in a heated exchange is different from a sustained campaign. We look at context, history, and pattern.

---

### 2. Harassment and Coordinated Pile-Ons

Harassment is unwanted, persistent, or threatening conduct directed at a specific person. Coordinated harassment is when multiple people act together — whether explicitly organized or through implicit signals — to target the same individual.

**What this looks like:**
- Sending repeated messages to someone who has not responded or has asked you to stop
- Rallying other members against a specific person ("she's the one who said X, go let her know")
- Replying to someone's posts across multiple threads with hostile or antagonistic content after they have indicated they want the interaction to stop
- Using the platform's resharing features to expose someone to a hostile audience

We treat coordinated attacks as a single event, not as many independent incidents. If ten people report the same account within a short period — particularly accounts that show a pattern of reporting people similar to themselves — we evaluate whether this is a coordinated attack on that account, not ten independent valid reports.

---

### 3. Sexual Harassment and Unsolicited Sexual Content

This platform has no tolerance for sexual conduct directed at a person without her consent.

**What this looks like:**
- Sending unsolicited sexual messages or images in DMs
- Sending explicit images — including genitalia, sexual acts, or graphic nudity — without being asked and without established consent from the recipient
- Making repeated sexual comments on someone's posts or profile after she has made clear she is not interested
- Rating or commenting on another member's body in a sexual way
- Demanding sexual attention or engagement in exchange for anything

"She didn't say no" is not consent. If you are not sure whether explicit content or conversation is welcome, it is not.

---

### 4. Stalking and Surveillance

Stalking is monitoring, following, or tracking another member in ways that would make a reasonable person feel frightened, harassed, or watched.

**What this looks like:**
- Systematically monitoring another member's posts, activity, or connections to gather information about her
- Using information shared on this platform to track or locate someone offline
- Visiting someone's profile repeatedly after she has blocked you, using a second account or a third party's account
- Sending messages through other channels after being blocked here, specifically referencing what she has posted on this platform

This applies even when no explicit threat is made. Surveillance is harm in itself.

---

### 5. Doxxing and Sharing Private Information Without Consent

Doxxing means publishing private, personally identifying information about someone without her consent, with the intent or likely effect of enabling harassment, threats, or harm.

**What this looks like:**
- Posting someone's home address, workplace address, phone number, or personal email
- Sharing someone's real name when she has not made it public on the platform
- Publishing financial information — account numbers, salary, debt — about another person
- Sharing medical or mental health information someone has not chosen to make public
- Outing someone's gender history, transition status, sexuality, or other sensitive personal information she has not publicly shared
- Reposting information scraped from other platforms to expose someone here

It does not matter whether the information was technically findable somewhere online. Aggregating and amplifying private information is itself the harm. We treat this as a serious violation.

---

### 6. Threats of Violence

A threat is any communication that would make a reasonable person fear for their physical safety — including conditional threats and statements of intent disguised as hypotheticals.

**What this looks like:**
- Direct statements: "I will hurt you," "you are going to regret this"
- Conditional threats: "if you keep posting about this, something bad is going to happen to you"
- Threats involving third parties: "there are people who would love to get their hands on someone like you"
- Posting about harming yourself in ways that appear designed to pressure another person
- Publishing content that includes violent imagery directed at a named individual

Threats are reported to law enforcement where appropriate. We do not treat them as a moderation matter only.

---

### 7. Hate Speech — Including Misogyny, Transphobia, and Racism

Hate speech is content that dehumanizes, attacks, or incites hatred against a person or group based on who they are.

**Misogyny is a violation of these guidelines.** This platform was built for women's safety, and content that dehumanizes women, promotes hostility toward women as a class, or treats women as inferior based on their gender has no place here. This includes content that promotes the subjugation of women, attacks women's rights and autonomy, or uses this platform to distribute material whose purpose is hostility toward women. Given what this platform is for, this is not a grey area.

**We are explicit:** transphobia is hate speech and it is a violation of these guidelines. This platform includes trans women fully and without caveat. Content that denies the womanhood of trans women, promotes their exclusion from women's spaces, or treats gender identity as a debate to be won is not acceptable here. This is not a grey area.

**What transphobia looks like on this platform:**
- Denying that trans women are women
- Misgendering trans members deliberately and repeatedly
- Promoting or sharing "gender critical" or TERF ideology framed as feminist analysis — this framing does not make exclusion acceptable
- Posting content designed to make trans women feel unwelcome, unsafe, or like their presence is contested
- Comparing trans identity to delusion, mental illness, or deception

**What racism looks like on this platform:**
- Using racial slurs or dehumanizing language
- Promoting white supremacist, ethnonationalist, or segregationist ideas
- Asserting the intellectual, moral, or civilizational inferiority of any race or ethnic group
- Making jokes that rely on racial stereotypes as their punchline

**Other protected characteristics:**
The same principle applies to hatred directed at members because of their religion, disability, national origin, socioeconomic status, age, or any other characteristic that defines who they are rather than what they do.

Disagreement and criticism are not hate speech. The line is dehumanization.

---

### 8. Impersonation

Impersonation is presenting yourself as someone you are not — a specific real person, a public figure, or the platform itself.

**What this looks like:**
- Creating an account with another person's name and photo to deceive others about who you are
- Presenting your posts as coming from a well-known person or organization when they are not
- Creating an account designed to look like an official United Feminist account

Parody accounts are permitted if they are clearly labeled as parody. If there is any reasonable chance someone could mistake the account for the real thing, it is not clearly labeled enough.

---

### 9. Non-Consensual Intimate Imagery (NCII)

This is among the most serious violations on the platform. Non-consensual intimate imagery — sometimes called revenge porn — means posting, sharing, or threatening to share sexual images of a real person without their consent.

**What this looks like:**
- Posting nude, semi-nude, or sexual images of a person without their permission
- Sharing images that were originally shared in a private context (a DM, a relationship) without the subject's consent
- Threatening to post intimate images as a means of control, coercion, or punishment
- Sharing AI-generated or digitally manipulated intimate images of a real person

Any report of NCII is treated as a high-priority escalation. The content is removed immediately upon verification, the account is suspended, and we will cooperate fully with law enforcement.

If you are a victim of NCII on this platform, contact us immediately at safety@unitedfeminist.com.

---

### 10. Spam, Scams, and Commercial Exploitation

This platform is a place for genuine human connection. We do not allow it to be used as a commercial distribution channel, a fraud vector, or a manipulation tool.

**What spam looks like:**
- Posting identical or near-identical content repeatedly across threads, profiles, or DMs
- Following and unfollowing accounts at high volume to gain followers
- Using automated tools or bots to post, like, or engage

**What scams look like:**
- Impersonating brands, organizations, or individuals to solicit money or information
- Promoting fake investment opportunities, pyramid schemes, or multi-level marketing
- Soliciting account credentials, personal data, or payment information from members
- Phishing links or fraudulent URLs embedded in posts or DMs

**What commercial exploitation looks like:**
- Using DMs to send unsolicited promotional messages to members who have not opted in
- Coordinating bulk purchasing, ticket scalping, or market manipulation using the platform

Genuine small-business owners, freelancers, and entrepreneurs are welcome here. The line is between authentically sharing what you do and using the platform as a spam channel.

---

### 11. Content Involving Minors

There is no tolerance for any content that sexualizes, endangers, or exploits minors.

**What this means:**
- Posting, sharing, or linking to child sexual abuse material (CSAM) — including AI-generated material — is a crime. We will immediately report all known or suspected CSAM to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC) and cooperate fully with law enforcement. The account is permanently banned.
- Sharing content that could be used to identify, locate, or contact a minor for harmful purposes
- Grooming behavior — building an online relationship with a minor for the purpose of sexual exploitation
- Detailed sexual content involving minors, even when presented as fictional

This platform is 18+ only. If you become aware of a minor using the platform, please report it immediately.

---

### 12. Evading Enforcement

If your account has been suspended or banned, you may not create a new account to get around it. If you are under a content restriction or warning, you may not use a second account to post content you could not post from your primary account.

**Admission abuse** is a specific form of enforcement evasion. In the vouching lane, this includes:
- Vouching for someone you know to be banned or recently removed for serious violations
- Selling, trading, or giving invite tokens to strangers for commercial gain
- Coordinating with others to flood the invite system to get around account limits

In the reviewed queue, it includes:
- Submitting false or misleading information about your identity or intentions in order to pass review
- Creating multiple applications under different contact details to work around a denial

The admission system's value depends entirely on its integrity. We treat abuse of it seriously.

## Reporting Something

When you report a post, a profile, or a DM, here is what happens:

**Step 1 — Your report is received.** You will get a confirmation. The reported content is flagged for review. If you need to protect yourself from it in the meantime, use the block button — blocking does not notify the person you are blocking.

**Step 2 — Automated review.** Most reports are assessed by our automated moderation systems first. High-confidence violations (known CSAM, obvious spam patterns, clear threats) are acted on immediately.

**Step 3 — Human escalation.** Serious cases, nuanced situations, and anything where the automated review is uncertain goes to a human reviewer. Appeals are always reviewed by a human.

**Step 4 — Action or dismissal.** If a violation is found, we take action (see Enforcement below). If no violation is found, we close the report. Either way, we will let you know the outcome.

**Confidentiality:** We do not tell the reported person who reported them. We may not be able to share the specific details of what action we took, but we will tell you whether action was taken.

**If you are in immediate danger,** please contact local emergency services. We are a social platform, not emergency responders.

## Enforcement

We do not believe in one-size-fits-all enforcement. Context matters, severity matters, and pattern matters. Here is how we approach it:

### The Enforcement Ladder

**Warning**
For first-time or minor violations where the harm is limited and the intent appears careless rather than malicious. We tell you what rule was violated, what the content was, and what to expect if it happens again. The content may or may not be removed.

**Content removal**
The specific post, image, DM, or thread is removed. Your account remains active. This applies to violations that clearly cross a line but do not require suspending you.

**Temporary suspension**
Your account is suspended for a defined period — typically 3 to 30 days, depending on severity and history. During suspension, you cannot post, interact, or use DMs. Your profile remains visible unless we also remove your public content.

**Permanent removal**
Your account is permanently closed. This is the outcome for:
- Any CSAM-related violation
- Non-consensual intimate imagery
- Serious, credible threats of violence
- Repeated serious violations after prior suspension
- Doxxing that results in or is clearly intended to cause real-world harm
- Coordinated harassment campaigns
- Ban evasion

Permanent removal means your account is closed, your invitations are cancelled, and future accounts created by you are subject to removal.

---

### Inviter Accountability

Every member who joined through the vouching process arrived because someone stood behind them. That accountability runs in both directions.

If someone you vouched for violates these guidelines, here is what it means for your account:

| What happened to your invitee | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Minor violation — warning only | No impact on you |
| Temporary suspension of 7 days or more | One invite slot removed from your next period; you will be notified |
| Permanent removal for a serious violation | All invite privileges frozen; your account is reviewed |
| CSAM or illegal content | Your account suspended pending investigation |

**If two or more people you personally vouched for are permanently removed for serious violations within any six-month period, your ability to invite new members is permanently ended.** Your account may continue — this is about whether we can trust your vouching judgment, not about penalizing you for someone else's choices.

This accountability structure is what makes the vouching system worth anything. Please vouch only for people you actually know and whose presence here you are genuinely willing to stand behind.

## Appeals

If you believe a moderation action was wrong, you can appeal. Every appeal is reviewed by a human.

To appeal, contact us at appeals@unitedfeminist.com within 30 days of the action. Tell us:
- Your account @handle
- What action was taken
- Why you believe it was a mistake

We will review the evidence, the context, and the original decision. We may ask you for additional information. We will tell you the outcome.

We will not reverse decisions out of persistence alone — sending the same appeal multiple times does not change the outcome. But a genuine appeal with new information or a compelling argument for a different interpretation will be considered seriously.

For permanent removals, appeals must be submitted within 30 days. After that, we consider the record closed.
